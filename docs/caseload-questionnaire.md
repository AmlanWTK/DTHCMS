# Endocrine clinic caseload — a short questionnaire

**Roughly 20 minutes. No patient data required.**

---

## Why you are being asked

We are building a clinic management system for a diabetes, thyroid and hormone practice in
Bangladesh. Before it can be tested properly it needs a few thousand **invented** patient
records — fictional people with plausible ages, diagnoses, laboratory values, prescriptions
and follow-up histories.

Invented records are easy to generate. Invented records that a clinician would find
_believable_ are not, and that distinction matters more than it sounds. If the test data is
wrong, the software gets tested against a population that does not exist: screens are laid
out for the wrong distributions, performance is measured against the wrong volumes, and
safety rules are exercised against values no real patient would present with. Every one of
those problems surfaces later, in a live clinic, as a surprise.

So we would rather ask someone who sees these patients.

## What is being asked for — and what is not

**Wanted:** your general impression of the shape of an endocrine caseload. Proportions,
typical ranges, what is common and what is rare.

**Not wanted, and please do not provide:** anything from a patient record. No extracts from
a management system, no spreadsheets, no individual cases from memory. Nothing here should
identify anyone, and nothing here requires looking anything up.

An answer like _"about two-thirds of the diabetes we see is type 2, and most present late
with an HbA1c around 9 or 10"_ is exactly the right kind of answer. Approximation is fine
and expected — the aim is plausibility, not precision.

**If a question is outside your practice or you would rather not estimate, leave it
blank.** A blank is more useful than a guess, because we will fill blanks from published
figures and mark them clearly as not yours.
